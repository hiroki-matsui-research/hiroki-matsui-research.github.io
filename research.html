Research

How can gravity be reconciled
with quantum theory?

Should spacetime itself be quantized — and if so, how? If not, how should quantum gravity be formulated, and how do quantum-gravitational effects appear at low energies?

One of the central challenges in modern physics is to reconcile general relativity with quantum mechanics. My research lies at the intersection of quantum gravity and cosmology, with particular emphasis on the origin of the universe, the quantum structure of spacetime, and the low-energy implications of quantum gravity. Rather than presupposing a complete ultraviolet theory, I pursue a complementary research program combining the effective field theory of gravity, swampland conjectures, modified theories of gravity, and quantum cosmology.

One component of this program is the low-energy effective field theory (EFT) of quantum gravity, including quantum field theory in curved spacetime and semiclassical gravity. Within this framework, I investigate how higher-curvature and higher-derivative operators, renormalization effects, and quantum corrections affect the stability and domain of validity of semiclassical spacetime descriptions. As a complementary top-down perspective, I use the swampland conjectures, as proposed consistency criteria for ultraviolet completion. My aim is to determine which low-energy EFTs can be consistently embedded in quantum gravity and to explore their implications for inflation, dark energy, multiverse, and quantum cosmology.

A major focus of my recent research is the nonperturbative definition and evaluation of gravitational path integrals. By combining the Lorentzian path integral with Picard–Lefschetz theory and resurgence, I determine the relevant complex saddle points and integration cycles and systematically incorporate both perturbative and nonperturbative contributions. These methods provide a controlled framework for formulating and comparing proposals for the wave function of the universe, including the Hartle–Hawking no-boundary proposal and the tunneling proposal, while addressing the contour ambiguities and convergence problems inherent in traditional Euclidean formulations. A closely related question is how—or whether—a smooth, homogeneous, and isotropic classical universe can emerge from an underlying quantum state, and how primordial quantum fluctuations become effectively classical.

My research also explores gravitational models and extensions beyond four-dimensional general relativity, particularly Hořava–Lifshitz gravity and Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity. Hořava–Lifshitz gravity provides a framework in which anisotropic scaling and higher-spatial-derivative operators can modify the ultraviolet behavior of gravity and stabilize cosmological wave functions near classical singularities. Jackiw–Teitelboim gravity, by contrast, serves as an analytically tractable two-dimensional laboratory for studying gravitational path integrals, boundary conditions, and functional determinants. In parallel, I use the Wheeler–DeWitt equation and Lorentzian path-integral methods to investigate the quantum structure of black-hole interiors and to examine whether quantum-gravitational effects can resolve or avoid cosmological and black-hole singularities.

Another central objective of my research is to connect quantum gravity with observation and experiment. I study nonclassical squeezed states and higher-order correlations of primordial gravitational waves, decoherence induced by gravitons in macroscopic quantum systems, and light-cone smearing caused by fluctuations of spacetime geometry. By translating these effects into concrete observables—such as nonclassical correlation statistics, loss of quantum coherence, and modifications of propagation or causal structure—I seek testable signatures in the cosmic microwave background, future gravitational-wave observations, and precision quantum-interference experiments. Ultimately, my goal is to establish a coherent bridge between the fundamental consistency conditions of quantum gravity and phenomena that can be probed through cosmology, gravitational-wave astronomy, and quantum technology.

Current programme

One question organises everything below: how does the quantum nature of the gravitational field become first calculable, then observable? The first two threads build the calculational side — the non-perturbative structure of the gravitational path integral, and the low-energy operators a consistent theory leaves behind. The last three follow it out to what can be measured: the smearing of the light cone, the loss of coherence it induces, and the quantum signature of a primordial gravitational-wave background. The aim is to move quantum gravity out of formal theory and into a subject with testable consequences.

01

Non-perturbative methods of quantum gravity

The Euclidean gravitational action is unbounded below in the conformal mode, meaning that the path integral used to define the wave function of the universe diverges. Complexifying it merely shifts the issue to the choice of contour. Starting from the Lorentzian path integral instead, I use Picard–Lefschetz theory and other non-perturbative methods, such as resurgence, to resolve the relevant complex saddles and Lefschetz thimbles. This allows me to resum the perturbative series into a wave function that carries its non-perturbative contributions with it, meaning that the contour is determined rather than assumed. I am extending this approach to non-trivial geometries, as well as to black hole formation and evaporation in exactly solvable lower-dimensional gravity.

02

Low-energy effective field theory of quantum gravity

Below the Planck scale gravity can be treated as an effective field theory, together with quantum field theory in curved spacetime and semiclassical gravity. I am working out how higher-curvature and higher-derivative operators, renormalisation effects and quantum corrections bear on the stability and the domain of validity of a semiclassical spacetime — and, from the top down, which low-energy theories the swampland conjectures allow to be completed at all, with the consequences that carries for inflation, dark energy and quantum cosmology.

03

Propagators and causal structure in a fluctuating spacetime

Green functions are singular on the light cone — a direct imprint of causal structure. If geometry fluctuates, so do geodesic distances and light arrival times, and the light cone is effectively smeared. I am computing how that modifies correlators at short distance, separating local corrections from the non-local noise that survives as an observable.

04

Gravitational decoherence as an open quantum system

Coarse-graining the unobserved gravitational degrees of freedom as an environment induces noise and dissipation in the reduced density matrix. I am working out when interference between spacetime histories is suppressed — classicalisation of perturbations in the early universe, a shift in coherence time in the laboratory.

05

Quantum nature of primordial gravitational waves

Primordial tensor modes are born as quantum fluctuations and evolve into strongly squeezed states, yet are usually treated as classical noise. With squeezed-state methods and higher-order correlations from quantum optics, I am building indicators that tell a quantum background from a classical one in the bands targeted by CMB polarisation and space interferometers.
